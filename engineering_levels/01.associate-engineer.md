# 01 — Associate Engineer

**Essence**: delivers well-defined tasks with feedback loops close by. Learning the system
faster than they break it.

## Scope

- Single tasks: a bug fix, a small feature inside an existing service, a migration step.
- Accountable for the correctness of ONE file/diff at a time, within an established design.
- Works on one team's codebase; needs a buddy/senior for anything that crosses services.

## System design competency

- **Reads designs, doesn't write them.** Can explain what an existing architecture does:
  where requests flow through, what the cache is for, why there are two stores.
- Can trace one request path through 2–3 services with guidance.
- Understands back-of-envelope basics: QPS, why storage matters, what "eventual consistency"
  means — at the level of the study cases' `01-requirements.md` + `02-scale-estimates.md`.
- Does not yet: choose between design alternatives, assess that a trade-off is wrong, design
  a new service boundary.

## Technical execution

- Writes correct, readable code in the team's language/framework and its idioms.
- Uses version control properly (no broken main, no secret commits, clean history).
- Writes a basic test for new logic; runs the test suite; can debug a failing test.
- Reads logs/metrics to find where a bug lives, with hints on where to look.

## Judgment signals

- Asks "why is this the way it is?" — and remembers the answer.
- Finishes a task including its tests, callers, and edge cases, not just the happy path.
- Signals early when a task turns out bigger than scoped — rather than silently thrashing.
- Learns from review without needing the same comment twice.

## Not expected

- Designing architecture, picking technologies, or defining scope for a project.
- Making trade-offs under load/perf requirements without direction.
- Mentoring others or owning on-call.
- Being blocked only by "I don't know what I don't know" is fine at this level.

## Growth to next level

Close the gap from task → feature: take a ticket from "here's the design" to "here is the
design, I checked alternatives, I've shipped it and it held up." The observable step-up:
a senior hands over scope, not designs.
