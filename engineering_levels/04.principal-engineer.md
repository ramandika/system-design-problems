# 04 — Principal Engineer

**Essence**: owns direction across systems and orgs. Decides *what gets designed*, not just
*how*. Leverage without headcount.

## Scope

- A technical domain or platform spanning multiple teams (e.g., "storage", "messaging infra",
  "all client APIs"), or the hardest problem in the org.
- Accountable for multi-year technical health: the org's systems should get simpler and
  cheaper to change because of their work, not despite it.
- Sets the design bar: their review is where the org's risky designs get caught or approved.

## System design competency

- **Frames problems**: takes "the timeline is slow" (Senior's input) and decides whether the
  right answer is a cache, a redesign of the fanout model, or a product change that removes
  the requirement — and can kill the wrong framing in a meeting.
- Designs at the portfolio level: which systems exist, which get deleted, which boundaries
  hold the org together. Understands that their trade-offs now include org cost, hiring
  market, and lock-in — not just QPS/latency/cost.
- Owns the *hard* trade-offs: consistency vs availability across regions, one platform vs
  per-team autonomy, build vs buy. These don't have correct answers on paper; the Principal's
  value is making the choice legible and defensible.
- Sees the second-order effects: a fanout-on-write model (see
  [Twitter deep dives](../study_cases/02.twitter/04-deep-dives.md)) is not just a write-path
  choice — it determines storage shape, cache sizing, deploy patterns, and what a team of 10
  can realistically own.

## Technical execution

- Still technical enough to detect fluff: reads the architecture, the code, and the graphs,
  and is right when they say "this won't hold".
- Writes the direction docs (strategy, principles, the "we don't do this because" list)
  others reference.
- Prototypes/de-risks the load-bearing unknowns personally when needed.
- Runs technical reviews and postmortems; raises the org's incident-to-design feedback loop.

## Judgment signals

- Decisions they made are written down and outlive the meeting: a principle doc others cite
  months later, a rejected framing left in a review thread, a "we stopped doing X because
  of Y" that traces back to them.
- Wins by argument in rooms they don't control; changes minds with evidence and framed
  choices, not with rank.
- Can point to a concrete decision that deleted work — a system retired, a class of
  problems designed away — and name what was traded to get it.
- Distinguishes "the system needs this" from "this team wants this" — and says which one
  out loud while making the call.

## Not expected

- Direct people management (hiring, growth, performance) — that's the [EM](05.engineering-manager.md)'s
  craft; a Principal's org leverage is technical, not managerial.
- Owning delivery of an arbitrary roadmap; they own direction, and delivery follows.
- Sprawling: a Principal whose influence doesn't concentrate anywhere is not a Principal.

## Growth to next level

Moving to EM swaps the currency: from *technical judgment leverage* to *people & outcome
leverage*. It is not "Senior, but more" or "Principal, but with reports" — it's a different
job, and going back should be allowed without stigma (see [05](05.engineering-manager.md)).
