# 03 — Senior Engineer

**Essence**: owns systems and their trade-offs. Designs beyond a single service, and is
accountable for the design's behavior under load and failure.

## Scope

- A whole system (several services + their data + their SLAs), or a team's technical area.
- Accountable for availability, latency, and cost of what they own — including the parts they
  didn't write themselves.
- Designs and reviews designs for the whole team; a Mid's design passes review or dies here.

## System design competency

- **Designs multi-service systems** with correct handling of: failure modes (what breaks
  first, what happens to the user), partial failure (one dependency down, not the system),
  and skew (my median ≠ my p99; celebrities in the
  [Twitter fanout problem](../study_cases/02.twitter/04-deep-dives.md)).
- Chooses between architecture patterns and defends the choice in writing: fanout-on-write vs
  read, sync vs async boundaries, one vs N stores, exactly-once layers.
- Does the scale math correctly and *acts on it*: can size a fleet, find that a plan dies at
  peak QPS, and restructure before shipping.
- Decomposes problems: turns a vague "the timeline is slow" into 2–3 bounded investigations
  with ownership per part.
- Does not yet (that's Principal): change what the problem is; design across org boundaries
  where incentives, not just tech, decide the answer.

## Technical execution

- Deep in ≥1 domain (storage, distributed systems, or the team's core product); conversant across the stack.
- Debugs production incidents: leads the investigation, not just a log search.
- Writes the design doc others implement from; reviews others' for the two or three things
  that will actually break.
- Builds the failure tests: chaos/load/degradation scenarios, not just happy-path CI.

## Judgment signals

- Designs for the failure case first, then the happy path.
- Distinguishes reversible from irreversible decisions and spends accordingly.
- Says "no" with a better alternative; kills bad designs (incl. their own) without ego.
- Knows the system's weak points without opening the code — from running it.
- Their code designs are boring, on purpose: novel only where novelty earns its keep.

## Not expected

- Owning cross-team org direction or tech strategy for multiple teams.
- Making people decisions (hiring calls, perf management) — that's EM territory; see [05](05.engineering-manager.md).
- Being correct on every call; being *accountable* on every call is the standard here.

## Growth to next level

Close the gap from system → portfolio: influence what gets designed across teams, own a
multi-year technical direction, and win by argument (not authority) in rooms they don't
run. The observable step-up: other teams' plans change because they were there.
